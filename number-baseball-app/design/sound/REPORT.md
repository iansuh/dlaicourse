## Method

- **Loudness**: ITU-R BS.1770-4 K-weighting (libebur128 filter constants, cross-checked against ffmpeg `ebur128`). *M-max* = highest loudness of any 400 ms window (sample hop, sound zero-padded so a short sound is measured whole); *S-max* = the same with a 3 s window; *I* = gated integrated loudness (files under 400 ms padded to one block). Effects are normalised and judged on **M-max**: for sounds of 30 ms to 2.5 s a 3 s short-term window mostly measures silence, so the 400 ms window is the meaningful 'short-term' figure for one-shots. Music is judged on I.
- **True peak**: 4x oversampled (polyphase), dBTP. **Clipping**: samples at |x| >= 0.999. **DC**: |mean| as a fraction of full scale.
- **Lead silence**: time to the first sample above -50 dBFS (rule for effects: < 5 ms). **End**: peak of the last 2 ms (rule: <= -50 dBFS, so nothing is cut off with a click).
- **Phone loss**: how much loudness disappears through a 2nd-order 300 Hz high-pass, a rough model of a phone speaker. Above 6 LU means the cue relies on bass a phone cannot play.
- **Loop seam** (music): the second difference |x[n+1] - 2x[n] + x[n-1]| spikes at a discontinuity. The seam figure is the largest value within 64 samples of the loop point (end joined to start), divided by the 99th percentile of the same statistic over 64-sample blocks of the whole loop; above 1.5 would be a click. Also checked: energy above 6 kHz in 5 ms around the seam vs. the 95th percentile of the loop (rule <= 2), and the loudness of the last vs. the first 400 ms (rule within 3 LU). Detector self-test: the same loop deliberately spliced at two unrelated points must score far above 1.5.
